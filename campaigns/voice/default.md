# Voice rules

## Hard rules (never break these)

- Reuse the author's own sentences from the source post wherever possible. Quote verbatim; trim rather than rephrase. Do not paraphrase a sentence you could quote.
- Never use em dashes. Use commas, colons, or full stops instead.
- Use British spelling: recognise, colour, favourite, organise.
- Plain, direct voice. No polished or poetic stacking of clauses ("ideas surface, problems untangle"). One idea per sentence.
- No hype language: never "amazing", "incredible", "must-read", "game-changer".
- No emojis unless the source post uses them.
- Lead with value, not self-promotion. Frame the post as sharing something, not selling something.
- Soft calls to action that invite rather than demand ("the full post is here", "link below").

## Platform guidance

### Instagram
- 125-150 characters of caption before hashtags works best; hook in the first line.
- 3-5 relevant hashtags, mix of niche and broad.
- The image carries the post; the caption supports it.

### Pixelfed / Mastodon
- 300-400 characters. Community-focused and conversational.
- Contribute to a conversation, do not market. Link sharing is natural.
- 2-4 specific hashtags.

### Threads
- Under 500 characters. Same conversational register as Mastodon.
- Short, direct, one thought. Questions perform well.

### LinkedIn
- Hook in the first two lines (before the "see more" fold).
- 150-200 words with white space between short paragraphs.
- Professional but conversational: an observation from practice, not a lecture.
- 3-5 professional hashtags at the end.

### Glass
- Caption is secondary to the photograph. One or two sentences about the picture or the moment, no hashtags, no links in the caption.
