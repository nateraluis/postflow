# Voice rules

## Hard rules (never break these)

- Every post must stand alone as a complete piece of writing, with its own opening hook, a body that delivers one idea properly, and a natural closing. A reader who never opens the link should still take something away.
- Write in the author's voice: plain, direct, first person. Borrow their distinctive phrases and images where they fit naturally, but never stitch quotes together into a collage. The copy must read as if the author sat down and wrote it for this platform.
- One concrete idea per post: a moment, a picture, a lesson, a number. Pick the strongest angle from the source, not a summary of everything in it.
- Never use em dashes. Use commas, colons, or full stops instead.
- Use British spelling: recognise, colour, favourite, organise.
- No polished or poetic stacking of clauses ("ideas surface, problems untangle"). One idea per sentence.
- No hype language: never "amazing", "incredible", "must-read", "game-changer".
- No emojis unless the source post uses them.
- Lead with value, not self-promotion. The post shares something; the link is there for whoever wants more.
- Weave the link in naturally near the end as part of a sentence ("the full story is on the blog: URL"), never as a bare "Full post: URL" bolted on.
- Soft calls to action that invite rather than demand.

## Platform guidance

### Instagram
- The image carries the post; the caption gives it context or a story.
- Hook in the first line (it shows before the fold). 2-5 short paragraphs is fine.
- Mention the link is in the bio, in a natural sentence.
- 3-5 relevant hashtags, mix of niche and broad.

### Pixelfed / Mastodon
- 300-480 characters. Conversational, like telling a friend one interesting thing.
- Open with the thought or moment, not the topic. Contribute to a conversation; do not market.
- A question or an invitation to try something lands well as the close.
- 2-4 specific hashtags.

### Threads
- Under 480 characters. Same conversational register as Mastodon.
- One thought, sharply put. A question as the closing line performs well.

### LinkedIn
- Hook in the first two lines (before the "see more" fold): a tension, a moment, or a plain surprising statement.
- 120-200 words in short paragraphs with white space. Tell the small story or lesson properly, then close with one takeaway or a question to the reader.
- Professional but personal: an observation from practice, not a lecture.
- 3-5 professional hashtags at the end.

### Glass
- Caption is secondary to the photograph. One or two sentences about the picture or the moment, no hashtags, no links in the caption.
